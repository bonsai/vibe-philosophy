# Set Theory and Venn Diagrams

## Purpose
Use sets to make category definitions and relationships explicit. Diagrams are explanatory models, not evidence by themselves.

## Procedure
1. Define the universe U and the scope that determines membership in it.
2. Define every set with a clear inclusion rule and source.
3. Identify candidate intersections, exclusions, subsets, unions, and complements.
4. Test each proposed relation against definitions and evidence.
5. Preserve unclassified, disputed, and unknown cases.
6. Draw a Venn diagram only after deciding which relations are justified.
7. Label the diagram with its scope, definitions, source, period, and uncertainty.

## Example schema
For a historical-status analysis:
- U: the explicitly defined population under study.
- C: people meeting the selected definition of citizen.
- S: people meeting the selected definition of enslaved.
- F: people meeting the selected definition of free.
- P: people permitted to participate in a specified political function.

Possible questions include whether C ∩ S is empty, whether C ∪ S = U, whether F is the complement of S, and whether P ⊆ C. None should be assumed without evidence and definitions.

## Boundaries
- Two circles do not prove the categories are exhaustive.
- A complement is always relative to a specified universe.
- An empty intersection or subset relation is a claim to establish, not a decorative choice.
- Set relations do not by themselves explain causation, power, legitimacy, or moral value.

## Verification
Check every relation against the membership rules. Ensure the diagram identifies the universe and accounts for exceptions and unclassified cases.
