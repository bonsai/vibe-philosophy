# Aristotelian Classification

## Purpose
Clarify what makes something a member of a category, how categories differ, and whether an institution's stated purpose matches its operation.

## Procedure
1. Define the object and scope of classification.
2. State the criteria for category membership.
3. Separate essential criteria from incidental features.
4. Identify causes, functions, purposes, and the evidence for each.
5. Test borderline cases and counterexamples.
6. Check whether categories overlap, omit cases, or depend on a specific constitution or context.
7. Separate descriptive claims from normative judgments.

## Historical care
Aristotle's *Politics* discusses citizenship in relation to participation in deliberative or judicial functions, with qualifications by constitution, and defends a theory of “natural slavery.” Present these claims in their historical and argumentative context; do not endorse them or project one timeless definition of citizenship onto all Greek poleis.

## Output
- Terms and definitions
- Membership criteria
- Causes/functions/purposes
- Borderline cases and exceptions
- Evidence and uncertainty
- Descriptive versus normative conclusions

## Boundaries
- A classification is only as sound as its criteria and evidence.
- Do not confuse an institution's stated purpose with its actual effects.
- Do not treat historical existence as moral justification.

## Verification
Try at least one counterexample or borderline case against each important category definition.
