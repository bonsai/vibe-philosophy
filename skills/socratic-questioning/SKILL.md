# Socratic Questioning

## Purpose
Test whether a claim is clear, coherent, and supported. The goal is not to win an argument or imitate an ancient dialogue, but to make assumptions and uncertainty visible.

## Procedure
1. State the claim in one sentence.
2. Define key terms in the context where they are used.
3. Separate observations, interpretations, assumptions, and value judgments.
4. Ask what evidence would support or weaken the claim.
5. Test the definition against ordinary, edge, and counterexample cases.
6. Check whether the reasoning assumes its own conclusion or changes the meaning of a term.
7. Revise the claim or mark the unresolved question.

## Output
- Claim under examination
- Working definitions
- Explicit assumptions
- Supporting and opposing evidence
- Counterexample or contradiction
- Revised claim and unresolved questions

## Boundaries
- Questions alone do not establish facts.
- Do not attribute a modern interpretation directly to the historical Socrates without source context.
- Do not force a conclusion when evidence is insufficient.
- Distinguish “not yet answered” from “unanswerable.”

## Verification
A second reader should be able to identify the claim, assumptions, and what evidence could change the conclusion.
